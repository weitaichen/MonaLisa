## 1. Shih et al. 2019 "Distortion-Free Wide-Angle Portraits on Camera Phones" — energy, solver, runtime; follow-ups 2020–2026

### Takeaway
Shih 2019 solves a **103×78 forward mesh** with face, grid-edge "line-bending", smoothness and boundary terms. It uses Ceres trust-region and takes 340 ms of a 920 ms, 12 MP pipeline on a Snapdragon 845. Its "line-bending" term is a **grid-edge shear penalty, not a detected-line constraint**. Follow-ups (Lai et al. 2021/22, Tan et al. 2021) found that background lines near the subject still bend. They added an explicit term built from detected line segments, with LSD as the detector, on a coarser 33×25 mesh. Industry did the same for 美型/美體: ByteDance (already in the earlier notes) and Kuaishou Y-tech (2021, new here).

### Cited Findings
**Shih, Lai, Liang (Google), ACM TOG 38(4) Art. 61, SIGGRAPH 2019** — [PDF (author copy)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf), [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3306346.3322948)
- Pipeline: subject segmentation → face mask → stereographic target mesh M_u from the camera FOV (EXIF) → energy-minimising mesh that follows M_u on faces and stays perspective on the background → resample. — [Shih PDF §3](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- Face weights w_i are **binary**: 1 if the uniform-grid vertex p_i falls in the face mask, 0 otherwise. The face mask is the subject segmentation intersected with expanded face boxes. A naive per-vertex interpolation between M_p and M_u "can show severe artifacts", and "propagating M_u outside the faces would introduce background distortion". This is why they optimise instead. — [Shih PDF Eq. 3, §3–4](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Face term** (per face k): E_s,k = Σ_{i∈B_k} w_i m_i ‖v_i − (S_k u_i + t_k)‖² + ws‖a_k − s_t‖². S_k = [[a_k, b_k], [−b_k, a_k]] is a per-face similarity and t_k a translation, both hidden variables discarded after solving. ws = 2000, s_t = 1. m_i is a radial sigmoid (1+exp(−(r−r_a)/r_b))⁻¹, set to 0.01 at the image centre and 1 at 100° FOV. — [Shih PDF Eqs. 5–8](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **"Line-bending" term** (taken from Chang et al. 2011): E_b = Σ_i Σ_{j∈N(i)} ‖(v_i − v_j) × e_ij‖², with e_ij the unit direction of p_i − p_j. It "penalizes the shearing of the grid, and therefore preserves the edge structure on the background". Without it, "rigid edges such as the window frames" bend (Fig. 7). — [Shih PDF Eq. 9](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Regularisation**: E_r = Σ_i Σ_{j∈N(i)} ‖v_i − v_j‖² over 4-neighbours. — [Shih PDF Eq. 10](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- The authors tried the grid line-bending regularisation of Wang et al. 2008. It "works effectively but introduces a non-linear op[timization]", which was too slow "for interactive usage on mobile platforms". — [Shih PDF §4](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Boundary**: the mesh is padded by q = 4 vertices per side. Boundary vertices may move only along their border (v_x = p_x on the left/right edges, v_y = p_y on the top/bottom). An extra term E_a uses indicator penalties to make the original boundary expand rather than shrink, which reduces undefined regions. The padded vertices are cropped after solving. — [Shih PDF Eqs. 11–13](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Total**: E_t = λ_f E_f + λ_b E_b + λ_r E_r + λ_a E_a with λ_f, λ_b, λ_r, λ_a = **4, 2, 0.5, 4**, fixed for all results. They were tuned sequentially on 20 photos and gave good results "after 2-3 iterations" of tuning. — [Shih PDF Eq. 14, §4.3](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Init and solver**: vertices are initialised by Gaussian kernel regression of the face displacements (bandwidth h = 2.37), which "speeds up the convergence". The constrained least-squares problem is solved with **Ceres** using a Trust Region minimiser with a large initial trust region. The output is then normalised by a scale and translation to avoid undefined regions. — [Shih PDF Eqs. 15–16, §4.3](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- **Mesh 103×78; device SDM845**. Segmentation runs on the Adreno 630 with TFLite. Warping uses a 7×7 Lanczos resampler in a GPU shader. **12 MP total 920 ms = segmentation 280 ms + mesh optimisation 340 ms + warping 115 ms**. Peak memory occurs at the warp, which needs one extra full-size framebuffer. — [Shih PDF §4.3](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- Lens-distortion correction is folded into the same warp field, so the image is resampled only once. — [Shih supplement §1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19_supp.pdf)
- **Limits**: "The curvature of an edge may change when it is very close to face regions" (Fig. 21). Of 4,131 photos, 7 (0.16%) failed from missing segments and 6 (0.1%) from "distortions on objects nearby the face". Correcting faces but not torso or arms can look inconsistent. User study (1,047 photos, 5 raters each): 92.4% judged more natural. — [Shih PDF §5–6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)

**Lai, Shih, Liang, Yang — "Correcting Face Distortion in Wide-Angle Videos" (arXiv 2111.09950, IEEE TIP 31:366–378)** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- They renamed Shih's term the "grid edge-bending term" and added a separate **line-preservation term**, because "the straight lines may be easily bent even with the regularization of the line-bending term in [4]" (Fig. 7). "Both … are necessary." — [Lai §IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- **Line term**: each detected segment l is cut by the quads it crosses. In quad q its piece is d_{l,q} = b − a, where a = q·w_a and b = q·w_b are the intersection points in the quad's bilinear (barycentric) coordinates, which stay fixed under deformation. E_l = Σ_l Σ_{q∈l} ‖d_{l,q} − s_{l,q} d̂_{l,q}‖². Here d̂ is the piece before warping, and s_{l,q} is a latent scale substituted by its optimum (d̂ᵀd̂)⁻¹d̂ᵀd. That makes E_l **quadratic** and penalises deformation orthogonal to the line. For video they "constrain the rotation of the line segments to be identical before and after warping". — [Lai Eq. 14, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- Lines come from **LSD** (von Gioi et al.). Endpoints are tracked with pyramidal LK. A line is dropped if the forward/backward reprojection error exceeds 2 px or its orientation changes by more than 1° between frames. Facial regions are excluded. — [Lai §III-A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- Weights: λ_f = 4, λ_s = 1, λ_e = 2, λ_b = 4 (spatial); **λ_l = 64**, λ_c = 4, λ_t = 16. Mesh **33×25** "for efficient optimization … coarser than … retargeting … sufficient". Solver: **LSMR (SciPy)**. — [Lai §III-D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- Timing (Python, Intel W-2135, 1024×768): face tracking and segmentation 63 ms, **line detection and tracking 37 ms**, **mesh optimisation 642 ms**, warping 99 ms, total 841 ms per frame, "without code optimization". — [Lai Table III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- **Failure**: "When a straight line is only partially detected, the undetected segment is not constrained … and may have a different orientation than the detected part" (Fig. 14). Correcting only the face leaves bodies unnatural (Fig. 13). — [Lai §IV-G](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)

**Tan et al. (Megvii), "Practical Wide-Angle Portraits Correction with Deep Structured Models", CVPR 2021** — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12464)
- "We notice that the edges near the faces are often distorted in [Shih]". To build ground truth they improved Shih "by adding explicit line constraints" (Lin et al. 2016, seamless video stitching). This "can alleviate the problem to some extent, but cannot be perfect". About half the images needed up to 5 reruns with re-tuned hyper-parameters, followed by a manual mesh tool combining **ARAP-quad (CPW) and line-preserving constraints**. — [Tan §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12464)
- They defined **LineAcc**: sample n points on each marked salient line and compare the per-step slope with the reference slope. They also defined ShapeAcc, from face-landmark vector cosines. In their Table 2, Shih's result scores LineAcc ≈ 66.143 / ShapeAcc 97.253 on their combined test set, versus ≈ 66.06 for the uncorrected input. *(The PDF table text extraction is misaligned, so treat the per-row numbers as approximate.)* — [Tan §5, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12464)

**Industry: Kuaishou Y-tech 美顏 "背景扭曲矯正" (2021-09-23, republished on qinglite)** — [qinglite repost](https://www.qinglite.cn/doc/418264769cf6b8e5d)
- They state that viewers notice background warping at a glance because lines that should be straight have bent. If those lines stay straight, small background shifts go unnoticed. The goal is therefore to keep background line **slopes** unchanged. — [Kuaishou Y-tech](https://www.qinglite.cn/doc/418264769cf6b8e5d)
- Method: 150 points in rings outward from the face contour (30 contour points; each connecting segment is split into 4), a background **triangle mesh**, and line detection on the original image with lines inside the face discarded. The objective is a **slope term** over m lines plus a **triangle shape-preservation term** "為了保持解的穩定" (for stability). It is solved as a non-linear problem (Gauss-Newton or LM are named, but which one ships is not stated). The image is then warped by the triangle mapping. — [Kuaishou Y-tech](https://www.qinglite.cn/doc/418264769cf6b8e5d)
- Shipped in 一甜相機 and 原片. It claims to "cover all phone models" and real-time preview, but gives **no ms figures** and no weights. It says only one competitor had this, and only for 小頭, while theirs covers all 美型/美體 operations. *(Unverified marketing claim. The fetched summary also implied body slimming was future work, so this is internally inconsistent.)* — [Kuaishou Y-tech](https://www.qinglite.cn/doc/418264769cf6b8e5d)

- Body-reshaping papers moved to learned flow or diffusion and still admit background damage. ### Inferences
- Shih's "line-bending" term written in displacement form d_i = v_i − p_i is a **shear penalty**. For a horizontal grid edge, (v_i − v_j) × (1,0) = −(d_i − d_j)_y. For a vertical edge it equals (d_i − d_j)_x. So E_b = Σ(∂D_y/∂x)² + Σ(∂D_x/∂y)². Those are exactly the derivatives that bend horizontal and vertical lines (the earlier notes state that a line bends iff the perpendicular component varies along it). E_b therefore protects axis-aligned structure **without any detector**, but not diagonals, and it is soft. This explains Lai's finding that it was insufficient near faces.
- Both mobile-shipping systems (Shih/Pixel, Kuaishou) solve a mesh far coarser than the image and apply it in one GPU resample. MonaLisa already has the "one resample" part (the RG16F field folded into the P2 reshape pass).
- Shih's 340 ms was for about 16k unknowns (103×78×2) with a non-linear trust-region solver in native code. A 33×25 to 33×44 **linear** problem (1.6k–2.9k unknowns) is 5–10× smaller and needs no iterations of the outer non-linear loop. That makes it the right size for a JS solve (see §2).

### Gaps
- No on-device ms figure exists for Lai's or Kuaishou's line-constrained solve. Lai's 642 ms is unoptimised Python including temporal terms. Kuaishou gives none.
- No 2023–2026 paper was found that adds an explicit line-preservation energy to **body** reshaping specifically. The literature is wide-angle and face correction, retargeting, rotation and stitching. The industrial body claims (ByteDance, Kuaishou) have no published energies or timings.

## 2. Mesh optimisation with straight-line constraints (CPW, line-preserving retargeting/rotation, SPHP, ASAP/ARAP) — formulation on a coarse grid, solver in JS, cost

### Takeaway
All of these methods share one linear template. A quad mesh carries (a) a data term that pins chosen points through bilinear weights, (b) an as-similar-as-possible shape term per quad or triangle weighted by salience, (c) a line term built from detected segments cut at quad borders that penalises each piece's component perpendicular to its original direction, and (d) boundary terms. With line rotation fixed (θ = 0), the problem is a single sparse SPD linear solve. Published sizes are 33×25 to 103×78. "Several hundred unknowns … a few milliseconds" in C++. In JS the realistic plan is a banded Cholesky factored once per photo with triangular solves per slider tick, or Jacobi-preconditioned CG. **No published JS/iPhone timing exists; the figures below are estimates.**

### Cited Findings
**Content-Preserving Warps, Liu, Gleicher, Jin, Agarwala, SIGGRAPH 2009** — [PDF](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)
- **Data term** E_d = Σ_k ‖w_kᵀV_k − P_k‖². Each constrained point is a bilinear combination w_k (4 weights summing to 1) of its enclosing cell's four output vertices. w_k comes from inverting the bilinear interpolation of the input cell. — [CPW §4.1.1](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)
- **Similarity term** (from Igarashi et al. 2005). Each cell is split into two triangles, and V1 should equal V2 + u(V3−V2) + vR90(V3−V2) with R90 = [[0,1],[−1,0]] and (u,v) fixed from the input. E_s(V1) = w_s‖V1 − (…)‖², summed over all 8 triangles touching each vertex ("redundant, but avoids special handling along the grid boundaries"). — [CPW §4.1.2](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)
- **Salience weight**: w_s = L2 norm of the colour variance in the cell, + 0.5 so every vertex stays constrained. — [CPW §4.1.3](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)
- E = E_d + αE_s is "a sparse linear system, where the matrix is narrow-banded since each vertex only shares constraints with its eight neighbors". It was solved with MATLAB backslash on a **64×36 grid**, about **0.5 s per frame**, and rendered by texture-mapping the mesh. — [CPW §4.1.6, §5](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)

**Content-Aware Rotation, He, Chang, Sun, ICCV 2013** (same group and formulation as the SIGGRAPH 2013 panorama rectangling) — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- Lines are extracted with **LSD (IPOL code)** and "cut by the input mesh grid, so each resulting line is within a single quad". Orientations are quantised into M = 90 bins; lines in one bin share a rotation θ_m. — [He ICCV13 §3.1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- **Line energy**: e_k = P_kV is the deformed piece, a linear function of the vertices via bilinear endpoint weights. E_L = (1/K) Σ_k ‖s_kR_ku_k − e_k‖². Eliminating s_k gives E_L = (1/K) Σ_k ‖(R_kU_kR_kᵀ − I)e_k‖² with U_k = u_k(u_kᵀu_k)⁻¹u_kᵀ, which is "quadratic on V". — [He ICCV13 Eqs. 2–3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- **As-similar-as-possible shape energy** (from Zhang et al.): E_S = (1/N) Σ_q ‖(A_q(A_qᵀA_q)⁻¹A_qᵀ − I)V_q‖². A_q is 8×4, built from the input quad corners as rows [x̂, −ŷ, 1, 0; ŷ, x̂, 0, 1]. — [He ICCV13 Eqs. 4–5](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- Boundary weight λ_B = 10⁸ (hard); λ_L = λ_R = 100. "Because V consists of several hundreds of unknowns, it takes only a few milliseconds to solve" the linear sub-problem. The non-linear θ step uses half-quadratic splitting. The bottleneck was bilinear image interpolation, about 0.5 s for 8 MP (C++, i7 3.0 GHz). — [He ICCV13 §3.2–3.3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- Treating all orientations strictly (equal weights for every bin) "respects all rotation, but at the price of larger distortion" (Fig. 6). — [He ICCV13 §3.4](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- A reimplementation of panorama rectangling ships with the line-preservation code present but disabled. — [VinceFior/panorama-rectangling](https://github.com/VinceFior/panorama-rectangling). RecRecNet authors note that line-preserving mesh deformation can "rotate the original scene and twist the object" (via search summary). — [RecRecNet supp.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/supplemental/Liao_RecRecNet_Rectangling_Rectified_ICCV_2023_supplemental.pdf)

**Shape-Preserving Half-Projective warps (Chang, Sato, Chuang, CVPR 2014)** — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/papers/Chang_Shape-Preserving_Half-Projective_Warps_2014_CVPR_paper.pdf)
- When one region is warped projectively and another by a similarity, "lines lying within R_H or R_L are kept straight", but the hard partition "introduces a sudden line bending at u = u1" for lines passing through it. The fix is a **buffer region** where the warp changes gradually from H to S. — [SPHP §3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/papers/Chang_Shape-Preserving_Half-Projective_Warps_2014_CVPR_paper.pdf)

**Solvers usable in a browser**
- **scijs/conjugate-gradient** (mikolalysenko): sparse SPD CG with a **Jacobi preconditioner**, CSR matrix and Float64Array right-hand side. Licence not checked. — [GitHub](https://github.com/mikolalysenko/conjugate-gradient)
- **scijs/cholesky-solve**: LDLᵀ sparse Cholesky, marked work-in-progress, licence not checked. — [GitHub](https://github.com/scijs/cholesky-solve)
- **linear-algebra-js / geometry-processing-js**: Eigen sparse Cholesky/LU/QR compiled to asm.js. MIT (GitHub API). Last push 2018. — [linear-algebra-js](https://rohan-sawhney.github.io/linear-algebra-js/), [repo](https://github.com/rohan-sawhney/linear-algebra-js)
- `eigen` npm (Eigen to WASM) 0.2.2 is about 3 years old and requires manual `delete()`. — [npm eigen](https://www.npmjs.com/package/eigen)
- For SpMV, the best JS browser was **2.2–5.8× slower than C**, WASM was "similar to or better than C", and double precision was often faster than single in JS. — [Sparse matrices on the web, ManLang '18](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3237009.3237020) (via search summary)
- In graphics mesh problems, sparse **direct** solvers beat multigrid, "in particular for problems with multiple right-hand sides". — [Botsch et al., Efficient Linear System Solvers for Mesh Processing](https://www.graphics.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/solvers1.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Template for MonaLisa (linear, θ = 0).** Unknowns: coarse-grid vertex values. Energy = Σ data(bilinear) + Σ_q ω_q·shape_q + λ_L Σ_k ‖(U_k − I)e_k‖² + λ_B·boundary. With θ = 0, (U_k − I)e_k is the component of the deformed piece perpendicular to its input direction. That is Shih's cross product applied to detected-line pieces instead of grid edges, and it is Lai's E_l after eliminating s. Everything stays linear, so one SPD solve suffices and no alternation is needed. Allowing rotation (He's θ bins) adds an outer loop. It is not needed for small (≤ 15%) body edits.
- **Size and cost estimate (unverified, by arithmetic).** A 33×44 node grid (portrait 3:4) has n = 2,904 unknowns with interleaved x/y. In row-major order along the 33-node side, the bandwidth is b ≈ 2·(33+1) ≈ 68. A banded Cholesky costs about n·b²/2 ≈ 6.7 M flops and stores n·b doubles ≈ 1.6 MB. A solve costs about 2·n·b ≈ 0.4 M flops. If JS runs at roughly 0.3–1 GFLOP/s scalar on an A-series core (an assumption), that is about **7–20 ms to factor** and **≤ 1 ms per solve**. Jacobi-CG with a warm start from the current field costs about 50k flops per iteration, so 100–300 iterations land in the same 5–15 M flop range. If only horizontal and vertical lines are constrained, x and y decouple into two n/2 systems with half the bandwidth, about 8× cheaper to factor.
- **Factor once, solve per tick.** If the weights depend only on the mask and the line set, not on slider strength, the system matrix is fixed per photo and only the right-hand side changes with the slider. This matches Botsch's "multiple right-hand sides" argument and the existing draft/full split in `buildBodyField` (draft = reuse the factor; full = re-sample lines and re-factor on release).
- Writing a dense-band Cholesky (about 60 lines of TS over a Float64Array) avoids every dependency and licence question. The available JS libraries are either work-in-progress, unlicensed, or from 2018.
- The SPHP finding is the general rule behind MonaLisa's feather: any spatial transition between "warped" and "identity" bends the lines that cross it unless the change is constant along each line. A wider feather only lowers the curvature. Only a line-aware term or field shape removes it.

### Gaps
- No measured runtime for a sparse or banded solve of about 3k unknowns in Safari/JavaScriptCore on iPhone was found. The 7–20 ms figure must be measured.
- He et al. 2013 rectangling (SIGGRAPH) PDF was not retrievable (author link 404). Its parameters (50 bins, λ_L = 100, λ_B = 10⁸, 10 iterations) are reported only from memory by a search summary and **are not verified**. The ICCV 2013 rotation paper above is the verified reference for the same formulation.

## 3. Straight-line detection usable in the browser (LSD, EDLines, ELSED, Hough, M-LSD, HAWP, DeepLSD): accuracy, speed, size, licences

### Takeaway
The best fit for an on-device PWA is a **classical detector ported to TS or WASM at about 384–512 px**. There are two licence-clean routes. One is OpenCV's restored LSD (`lsd.cpp`, now carrying OpenCV's BSD-3 header). The other is EDLines (ED_Lib, MIT) or ELSED (Apache-2.0), which are 2–10× faster than LSD. Avoid the IPOL reference LSD and the `line-segment-detector` npm package, which are AGPL. M-LSD (Apache-2.0, 1.28 MB fp16 TFLite) is the only learned detector small enough. It would need a second ML runtime, though, and its native memory footprint is 78–508 MB, so it is not worth it for this job. HAWP and DeepLSD (MIT) are desktop-GPU class.

### Cited Findings
**LSD (Grompone von Gioi et al., TPAMI 2010)**
- IPOL reference code v1.6 is **AGPL-3.0-or-later**. The abstract describes it as a "linear-time Line Segment Detector giving subpixel accurate results". — [IPOL](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2012/gjmr-lsd/)
- OpenCV removed the implementation in 3.4.6–3.4.15 and 4.1.0–4.5.3 "due original code license conflict". It was restored in **4.5.4** after the NFA computation code was published under MIT. — [OpenCV LineSegmentDetector docs](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/javadoc/org/opencv/imgproc/LineSegmentDetector.html) (via search summary), [opencv_contrib #2016](https://github.com/opencv/opencv_contrib/issues/2016) (issue only flags the AGPL problem)

- **Recommended: port OpenCV's `lsd.cpp` (BSD-3) to TypeScript, or build ELSED (Apache-2.0) or ED_Lib (MIT, after stripping the OpenCV types) to WASM.** Run it on the luma of the 384–512 px analysis image the editor already has. Assume about 20–100 ms in C at 512² (above), and 2–6× that in plain JS per the ManLang ratio. That is acceptable once per photo in a worker, but not per camera frame. EDLines or ELSED would be about 5–15 ms native.
- M-LSD is architecturally attractive (1.28 MB, Apache-2.0), but MediaPipe tasks-vision 0.10.35 has no task API that returns its TP maps. It would therefore need TF.js/tfjs-tflite or ONNX Runtime Web, self-hosted under `connect-src 'self'`, which adds several MB of runtime and a third model in memory on a 100–200 MB budget. Its learned "wireframe" bias (ignoring texture lines) is a plus, but the length and mask filters in §4 recover most of that with LSD. *(Licence of the Wireframe training dataset not verified.)*
- Hough (opencv.js) gives infinite lines or noisy segments and needs a full opencv.js download. It is not worth it next to LSD or EDLines.
- With every detector, apply these filters: segment length ≥ ~5% of the long edge; drop segments with any sample inside the person mask dilated by 1–2 field texels (silhouette edges of straight legs and arms, and clothing stripes, must *not* be protected); keep only segments that intersect the region where |field| > ~0.25 output px (others are trivially satisfied); cap at about 64–128 segments by length.

## 4. Turning detected lines into constraints compatible with a backward displacement field; blending with the person mask

### Takeaway
There are two workable formulations. **(A) Backward "sample-and-fit" residual solve.** Keep the current rule-based field as is. Solve a small coarse-grid **correction** field C so that, along each detected source line, the line-normal component of the total backward displacement is constant (parallel shift) or affine (small rotation). C is held at about 0 inside the person mask. It is linear and plugs into `localField()` with no GPU change. **(B) Forward mesh (Shih/CPW style).** Invert the rule field at the mesh vertices by fixed-point iteration (< 10 iterations), solve a forward mesh with the exact He/Lai line term, then **rasterise the deformed mesh into the RG16F field texture** so WebGL2 produces the backward map. Both keep the mask: as a high-weight data region inside the person and as the existing protect weight outside.

### Cited Findings
- Mesh-based methods render the result by texture-mapping the deformed mesh, which is a backward lookup done by the rasteriser. — [CPW §4.1.6](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf), [Shih §4.3 (GPU shader resampler)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- Line pieces are expressed in each quad's fixed bilinear coordinates, so constraints are linear in the vertices. — [Lai Eq. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950), [He ICCV13](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- Arbitrary point constraints enter the same way via inverse bilinear weights. — [CPW Eq. 1](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fliu/papers/siggraph09.pdf)
- **Inverting a displacement field**: Chen et al.'s fixed-point method builds the inverse by repeatedly evaluating the forward field. It "usually needs fewer than ten iterations" and was about 10× faster than ITK's iterative approach. Convergence is guaranteed under a Lipschitz condition, and the Jacobian should be checked first. — [Chen et al., Med. Phys. 35(1):81–88, 2008](https://aapm.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1118/1.2816107). *(The update rule v_{n+1}(x) = −u(x + v_n(x)) is the standard form given by the search summary from general knowledge; not read in the paper text.)*
- Industry places constraint points **outside** the subject (rings from the face contour outward) and optimises only the background mesh. — [Kuaishou Y-tech](https://www.qinglite.cn/doc/418264769cf6b8e5d). Shih uses the subject mask to decide where the face target applies and lets the background follow perspective, with E_b/E_r spreading the transition. — [Shih §3–4](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- MonaLisa today: the local field L (capsules and similar, Q units) is multiplied by the protect weight M (1 on the person dilated by Δ, smooth falloff over 4Δ, then 2× 3×3 box blur, forced to 1 inside), then `keepInFrame`. The bands g(y) are composed outside it: D = (0, g(y) − y) + L(x, g(y)), followed by a numeric det J ≥ 0.35 guard that scales L back. — `src/body/field.ts` (`buildBodyField`, `localField`), `src/body/mask.ts` (`protectWeights`)

### Inferences
**Why the backward constraint is "n·D affine along the line" (derivation).** A source line is ℓ = {x : n·x = c}. Under the backward map B(y) = y + D(y), its output image is the preimage {y : n·y + n·D(y) = c}. If n·D is constant along that curve, the preimage is the parallel line n·y = c − const. If n·D is affine in arc length, the preimage is a slightly rotated straight line. Otherwise the line bends. For small edits (|D| ≲ a few % of the frame), the preimage lies within |D| of ℓ, so it can be sampled at y_j ≈ x_j − D(x_j) (one fixed-point step), or simply at y_j = x_j when D is under one coarse cell. Error is second order.

**Formulation A — residual solve on the backward grid (recommended first).**
- Let F = M·L be the current protected local field (fine, 192×256). Unknown: a correction C on a coarse output grid (e.g. 25×33 or 33×44 nodes), bilinearly upsampled. Final local field: L' = F + C, then `keepInFrame`, the band composition and the det J guard as today.
- Energy, all terms linear in C:
  - E_data = Σ_i ω_i‖C_i‖². Use ω_i ≈ 1 (large) on nodes inside the person mask, so the body edit is preserved exactly. Use ω_i ≈ 10⁻² in the background, so C stays small where no line asks for it. Use ω_i large on other people's masks.
  - E_smooth = λ_s Σ_{4-nbr} ‖C_i − C_j‖². Optionally add Shih's shear form λ_e Σ[(ΔC_y along x)² + (ΔC_x along y)²], which also protects undetected horizontals and verticals.
  - E_line = λ_l Σ_ℓ Σ_j (n_ℓ·(F(y_j) + C(y_j)) − m_ℓ)². Here F(y_j) is sampled from the fine field (known), C(y_j) = Σ β_jk C_k (bilinear), and m_ℓ is a latent per-line shift. Eliminate m_ℓ by centring: subtract the mean over j. For the "allow small rotation" variant, project out an affine function of arc length instead. Sample spacing is about one coarse cell.
  - E_frame = λ_b Σ_border (C·ν)².
- Starting weights by analogy with the literature: line ≫ smoothness (Lai λ_l = 64 vs λ_s = 1; He λ_L = 100 vs E_S = 1). Normalise per line by its sample count so long lines do not dominate.
- Only lines whose normal has a component along the displacement are active. Horizontal lines are untouched by pure-x slimming, and verticals by y-only edits. So for typical 瘦腰/瘦腿 cases the constraints that bind are near-vertical background lines (door frames, wall corners, window edges), mainly in x. That system is small and nearly decoupled.
- Because ω and the line set do not depend on slider strength (fix sampling at y_j = x_j, or refresh on drag end), factor once per photo and solve per tick (§2). The fine field F keeps capsule detail; C is low-frequency by construction.
- After solving, run the existing `fieldMinJacobian` guard. A large λ_l can push compression elsewhere and fold.

**Formulation B — forward mesh plus rasterisation (exact line term).**
- Use a uniform source grid (e.g. 33×44). For each vertex p_i, compute the forward target f_i = p_i + u_i, where u inverts the backward rule field by fixed-point iteration (about 3–10 iterations, about 1.5k points, under 1 ms).
- Minimise Σ ω_i‖v_i − f_i‖² (data, high weight inside the person) + ASAP or CPW similarity per quad weighted by salience (colour variance + ε) + λ_l Σ_k ‖(U_k − I)e_k‖² over LSD pieces cut at quad borders + border sliding.
- Rasterise the deformed mesh into the RG16F field FBO with the per-vertex attribute (p_i − v_i) in UV. Interpolated over each output triangle, this gives the **backward** displacement exactly (piecewise-linear inverse), with no fixed-point step at render time. WebGL2 with `EXT_color_buffer_float` (100% iOS coverage per the earlier brief) can render into RG16F. A fold shows up as negative triangle area and can be checked on the CPU before upload.
- B matches the published methods one to one. Its line term is exact rather than linearised. It costs one extra draw call, and the capsule detail inside the body becomes piecewise-linear at mesh resolution. Mitigate this by rasterising only the correction (as in A) or by using a finer mesh inside the mask.

**Blending with the person mask.**
- Inside the person: high data weight (A) or high-weight targets (B), so the body edit is untouched.
- Silhouette ring: keep today's dilate-Δ, feather-4Δ protect weight as the *initial* F. The solve then redistributes its residual along lines instead of across them. A door frame touching the ring is translated as a whole, and its stretch is moved onto the untextured wall in between (the CPW salience logic).
- Lines crossing behind the person: link collinear segments on both sides of the mask (|Δθ| < ~2°, perpendicular offset < ~1–2 px at analysis resolution, gap overlapping the mask) into **one** line with one shared m_ℓ. This forces collinearity across the person, which addresses Lai's partial-detection failure.

**Cheapest detector-free option (C) — anisotropic protect.** Outside the mask, replace the isotropic field with D_x smoothed along y and D_y smoothed along x (long 1-D normalised-convolution kernels on the 192×256 field, under 1 ms), then blend by M. This is the explicit-filter analogue of Shih's E_b. Horizontal and vertical lines stay nearly straight; diagonals are not helped. Risk: displacement leaks along columns into background next to the head, arms or legs, causing stretch there. That is why the LS version (A) with the person as a high-weight data region is preferable. Prototype C first, because it reuses `protectWeights` and needs no detector.

### Gaps
- No published method was found that formulates line preservation directly on a **backward** displacement grid. Formulation A is derived here and must be validated: a grid test image with door frames, a numeric straightness metric such as Tan's LineAcc on synthetic lines, and the det J guard.
- The Kuaishou slope objective is non-linear (slope is a ratio). The cross-product/projection form used here is linear. No source compares their quality directly.

## 5. Known failure modes and limits (curved lines, textures, lines crossing the body), plus concrete recipes

### Takeaway
Line-aware warps fix the most visible tell, straight architectural edges near the subject. They do **not** fix curved structures, dense textures, partially detected lines, diagonals crossing the 長腿 band transitions, or detector and mask errors. Published systems still show residual bending near the subject (Shih Fig. 21; Tan: "cannot be perfect"). The approach is photo-editor-only: video needs line tracking and temporal terms (Lai), or it flickers.

### Cited Findings
- Edge curvature changes very close to the subject. 0.1% of 4,131 photos failed from distortion of objects near the face, and 0.16% from missing segmentation. — [Shih §6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/wide_angle_portrait/shih_sig19.pdf)
- A partially detected line keeps an unconstrained remainder that can change orientation. — [Lai §IV-G, Fig. 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- For video, per-frame mesh solves "create severe temporal flickering and wobbling". Lai needed endpoint tracking (≤ 2 px reprojection, ≤ 1° change), plus temporal (λ_t = 16) and coherence (λ_c = 4) terms. — [Lai §I, §III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09950)
- Explicit line constraints added to Shih "can alleviate the problem to some extent, but cannot be perfect". About half the images needed reruns with re-tuned hyper-parameters. — [Tan §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.12464)
- Constraining all line orientations strictly increases the distortion elsewhere. — [He ICCV13 Fig. 6](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2013/papers/He_Content-Aware_Rotation_2013_ICCV_paper.pdf)
- Line-preserving mesh warps can twist objects and rotate scenes in non-linear scenes. — [RecRecNet supp.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/supplemental/Liao_RecRecNet_Rectangling_Rectified_ICCV_2023_supplemental.pdf) (via search summary)
- EDLines can miss long lines in some scenes. — [ACM ICCVIT study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3627341.3630363) (via search summary)
- Lines in hair and unfitted regions are treated as background in whole-image triangulation methods. — [NeuralReshaper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10496) (already in earlier notes; cited only as the mask-error case)

### Inferences
- **Curved lines** (arches, round mirrors, car roofs): LSD splits them into short pieces. A per-piece "keep direction" constraint preserves local shape, but the length filter usually drops those pieces, so curves stay protected only by the isotropic feather.
- **Textures** (brick, tiles, blinds, fabric): many short parallel segments over-stiffen the solve and push distortion into odd places. Mitigate with the length threshold, a segment cap, and lower weight where segment density is high. The CPW variance salience already treats textured cells as stiff, which is often the right outcome.
- **Lines crossing the body**: handle with collinear linking across the mask (§4). Never constrain lines inside the mask (clothing stripes, belts), because the body must deform. Never constrain the silhouette's own straight edges (the dilated-mask test).
- **Line hugging the silhouette** (door frame at the waist edge): the data term (body edit) and the line term conflict. With ω_person ≫ λ_l, the frame is translated along its whole length, so the background between the frame and the body is compressed or stretched uniformly. If the frame is *occluded* by the moving edge, some bending is unavoidable. That is acceptable at the commercial 7–15% width change.
- **長腿/增高 bands**: y-only bands keep horizontals and verticals straight by construction. A diagonal crossing a transition can be kept straight only by adding a compensating x-shift that depends on its slope, and two crossing diagonal families (floor tiles) demand contradictory shifts. So the line solve can only reduce this; it cannot remove it. A cheaper alternative is to place the band transitions in rows with no detected diagonals.
- **Multiple people**: put other people into the high-weight data region with zero target, not into the line set.
- **Detector or mask errors** degrade gracefully to today's behaviour if the solve's correction C is bounded (clamp |C| to ≤ Δ) and the det J guard stays in place.
- **Live camera**: do not attempt. It would need per-frame detection, tracking and temporal terms, which is exactly the cost Lai paid.

### Recipes (synthesis of all five questions; numbers are starting points to tune, not sourced constants)
1. **R1 — anisotropic 背景保護 (no detector, under 1 ms; ship first).** In `localField()` after computing gx/gyy: outside `inside`, compute gx' = normalised-convolution of gx along **y** and gyy' = the same along **x**, with σ ≈ 0.15–0.25 of image height or width and weights = (1 − M). Then set L = M·L + (1 − M)·(L'·M_ring), where M_ring is a wider feather (e.g. 8Δ), so the displacement spreads along columns and rows rather than across them. Keep `keepInFrame` and the det J guard. Test: door-frame and tile photos at full 瘦腰/瘦腿; compare curvature of vertical lines and the stretch next to the head and arms.
2. **R2 — line-aware residual solve (editor, one photo per worker task).**
   - Line detection: port OpenCV `lsd.cpp` (BSD-3) to TS or WASM, or build ELSED (Apache-2.0) / ED_Lib (MIT) to WASM. Run at 384–512 px luma, once per photo (cache with the `BodyMeasure`). Filters: length ≥ 5% of the long edge; drop segments touching the person mask dilated by 2 texels; keep segments within about 2·feather of the silhouette where |F| > 0.25 output px; link collinear pairs across the mask; cap at 64–128.
   - Grid and solve: a 25×33 (Lai-size) or 33×44 correction grid. Data ω = 1 inside the person and other people, 10⁻² elsewhere. λ_s = 1 with optional Shih shear λ_e = 2. λ_l ≈ 32–100 per line normalised by its sample count. Border sliding. Line samples about 1 cell apart, at y_j = x_j.
   - Solver: a banded Cholesky (own TS, Float64Array) factored once per photo plus mask plus line set, with a back-substitution per slider tick, or Jacobi-CG warm-started from the last C (scijs/conjugate-gradient or about 40 lines of TS). Upsample C bilinearly and add it to F, then `keepInFrame`, the bands and the existing det J ≥ 0.35 guard (scale C back if it trips). Expected cost (estimate): 10–30 ms per factor and ≤ 1 ms per tick, plus 40–200 ms of JS LSD once per photo. Memory a few MB.
3. **R3 — forward-mesh variant (if R2's linearisation shows artefacts).** Fixed-point-invert the rule field at 33×44 source vertices (≤ 10 iterations, Chen 2008). Solve a CPW/He mesh with the data, salience-weighted ASAP, line Σ‖(U_k − I)e_k‖² and border terms. Draw the mesh into the RG16F `uBodyDisp` FBO with attribute (p − v), which needs one WebGL2 draw. Check signed triangle areas for folds.
4. **Not recommended now:** M-LSD (needs ORT-Web or TF.js plus ≥ 1.3 MB model, 111+ MB native memory at 320 px on A14), HAWP or DeepLSD (desktop-GPU class), learned flow or diffusion (earlier notes: licence and size), and any line-aware path in the live camera.
5. **Validation:** synthetic grid and door-frame images plus about 20 real full-body photos with architecture. Metric: Tan-style LineAcc on detected lines before and after, max det J, and stretch ratio next to the head and arms. A/B against the current feather-only 背景保護.
